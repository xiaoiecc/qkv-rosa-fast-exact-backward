# Exact backward from causal endpoints and query runs

For one stream of length $n$, let $J$ count the causal pairs $(t,r)$ with $r<t$ and Hamming distance at most one between the packed symbols $Q_t$ and $K_r$. Let $E$ count only the pairs with $Q_t=K_r$, so $E\le J$. Both counts concern only the last symbol. Let $b$ be the symbol width and $d$ the dimension of a row score. At fixed $b,d$ the complete singleton credit takes

$$
\widetilde O\big(\min(n+E,n^{5/3})\big)
$$

time. An independent bound of $O(n\log^2(2n))$ time and $O(n)$ space applies whenever $Q_1,\ldots,Q_{n-1}$ are constant, for arbitrary keys, payloads, and gradients. A suffix certificate extends the time bound to certain nonconstant queries. The all-input space bound is still $\widetilde O(n^{4/3})$. A fixed joint-pair budget contributes another $\widetilde O(n)$ for edited forwards and proposals. For a growing pair budget $p$, retain the $\widetilde O(pn)$ term and the proposal costs. These arithmetic bounds cover a supplied diagonal row cost as well as first-order credit. The implementation uses binary64, including the FFT.

## Count the alignments that could matter

Write $F(r)$ for the exact suffix length of row $t$ ending at $r$, with lexicographic winner $(L,r_*)$ ordered by length, then latest end. For a legal endpoint put $a=F(r)$. Its first mismatch to the left is at $(u,s)=(t-a,r-a)$. A repair exists exactly when $s\ge0$ and $Q_u\oplus K_s$ is the edited bit. Its length is $a+1+\operatorname{LCSuffix}(u-1,s-1)$. This one calculation supplies both a Q candidate and a K candidate. Every repair has either a matching last symbol or its only mismatch at the last symbol. This proves the coverage of $J$. Crossing the edited word and avoiding it partition the flipped matches into repairs and survivors.

For Q deletion put $h=t-u$. A survivor has length at most $h$. For $0<h<L$ its latest eligible endpoint is $R(h)=\max\{r:F(r)\ge h\}$. At zero the route is empty, and at $h\ge L$ the ordinary winner survives. Scanning exact endpoints from latest to earliest gives the entire staircase $R$ in at most one step per endpoint. Add the score of each plateau to an interval of Q owners with two difference-array writes.

For K deletion only $r_*-L<s\le r_*$ needs attention. Let $A_s$ be the prefix maximum of $(F(r),r)$ for $r<s$. Among the remaining endpoints a length $h>0$ survives exactly when $R(h)-h\ge s$. This quantity is strictly decreasing in $h$. In a staircase step $[a,b]$ with endpoint $r$, its largest surviving height is $\min(b,r-s)$ if $r-a\ge s$. Merge the breakpoints of the prefix maximum and the last surviving step as $s$ increases. Between two breakpoints the preferred endpoint switches at most once, because $A_s$ is fixed and $\min(b,r-s)$ is nonincreasing. Equal positive lengths go to the later endpoint. This emits $O(1+e_t)$ constant-endpoint ranges in a row with $e_t$ reported endpoints, even when $L=\Theta(n)$. Group repairs by edited coordinate, compare the best to its surviving route, and add its score difference.

The one-bit census looks up each query and its editable one-bit neighbors against a running histogram of earlier keys. Small alphabets use direct addressing. Otherwise full-word ranks use bounded probes with sorted overflow. Wide words first try an index omitting one bit band at a time, verify whole words and abandon crowded buckets after $12n$ visits. When that fails, a second exact census accepts up to $12n$ true pairs and regenerates their rows from sorted occurrence lists. Both attempts take $\widetilde O(n)$ even on adversarial projections and hash collisions.

### Remove one-bit fanout from the complexity bound

A repaired alignment of length at least two was exact at its last or its penultimate symbol. A row therefore needs only its current equal endpoints and the preceding row's equal endpoints translated by one. Exclude already-equal last symbols in the second list. Across the whole stream this visits at most $2E$ endpoints. The remaining task is to account for length-one repairs, even if $J$ is quadratic.

* For Q, a length-one repair edits the last query symbol. Its best candidate is the latest causal occurrence of the changed symbol. Seed that owner and bit with this route, then compare the longer candidates against this seed. Other Q owners use their usual deletion route.
* For K, the length-one repair at $s$ returns $s$. If $L\ge2$ and $s$ is outside the winning occurrence, the original winner survives. If $s$ lies inside it and $K_s\ne Q_t$, then $s<r_*$ and the tail ending at $r_*$ has positive length. Even at length one its later end defeats $s$. Thus the one-symbol seed matters only on rows with $L\le1$. There it wins precisely when $s$ is later than the baseline endpoint and its flip creates equality. All rows of a given query symbol satisfying this condition for a key edit form an interval in that class's occurrence list up to the next unedited matching key. The existing one-symbol control sums the interval using weighted prefix differences.
* Run the shared one-symbol repair control with weight one on $L\le1$ rows and zero elsewhere. On longer rows add just the Q seed. The row sweep supplies full deletion, and emits the difference between the best longer repair and the seeded survivor only when the longer repair wins. This decomposition also works for the diagonal score because each row's costs are evaluated relative to its true original output.

A running count per key class computes $E$ in $O(n)$ after building the one-symbol plan. The implementation streams the two equality lists when $E\le W=n\max(1,\lfloor n^{2/3}/128\rfloor)$. Sorting the per-row records and querying exact suffix lengths costs $\widetilde O(n+E)$ overall and $O(n\log(2n))$ space at fixed width, including the existing suffix RMQ. If $E>W$, then $n^{5/3}=O(E)$ and the previous $\widetilde O(n^{5/3})$ general algorithm applies. All earlier certificates either succeed in near-linear time or reject within that budget. This proves the stated minimum bound. When all query words have even parity and all key words have odd parity, $E$ can vanish while $J$ is quadratic. Conversely, long delayed copies can have $E=O(n)$ while the sum of winning lengths is quadratic. Neither one-bit fanout nor long matches force a quadratic traversal in this algorithm.

## Sum every edit against a repeated query word

Let the effective query be the word $x$ repeated. Row zero cannot look up a key. A baseline winner is the longest, then latest, run of $x$ in the available key prefix. Carry its two best finished runs and the currently growing run.

* Deleting $K_s=x$ inside the winner $[f,r]$ leaves three contenders. The left fragment has length $s-f$ and endpoint $s-1$, the right fragment has length $r-s$ and endpoint $r$, and the best intact rival has a fixed length and endpoint. Comparing these affine lengths and their ties gives a constant number of owner intervals per row. A difference array sums row weights before one dot product with each left endpoint's payload.
* Turning a one-bit neighbor $K_s$ into $x$ joins its adjacent runs. Until the joined run ends, its length grows by one per row and its endpoint is $t-1$. Afterwards its length and endpoint are fixed. The ordinary winner is nondecreasing and grows in length by at most one per row. Two binary searches therefore find the winning part of each phase, including endpoints tied in length. Prefix sums of fixed payload projections, the aligned projection at $t-1$, and the ordinary score evaluate both intervals.

For a Q edit at $u$, write $h=t-u$. A crossing repair uses a source $s<u$ with $K_s=x\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}2^j$ followed by at least $h$ copies of $x$. Its length is $h+1+\ell_s$, where $\ell_s$ is the preceding run length, and its endpoint is $s+h$. For each age the maximum therefore prioritizes $(\ell_s,s)$. Any crossing repair exceeds the deletion cap $h$. For a surviving match of positive height $h$, retain the latest run of length at least $h$ starting at or before $u$. Later runs starting after $u$ cannot supply $h$ symbols before $t$. When the latest qualifying run is still growing, clip its endpoint to $t-1$. If no run qualifies the original winner survives. At age zero deletion returns empty.

Sweep $u$. Record a change of endpoint at age $h$ only when a run starts or a new one-bit source becomes eligible. Per bit the lengths of the right arms charged to these insertions sum to $O(n)$, since each nonempty arm is a distinct run. This also bounds the sum, over epochs, of one plus the largest age touched in each epoch. Correct the clip on an unfinished run with one aligned prefix sum per owner, beyond the maximum age reached by eligible repairs.

A change at age $h$ remains in force for later owners and acts on numerical row $u+h$. Split the epochs recursively. At each split, sum all changes in the left half into one age-indexed vector kernel, then correlate it with the row weights for the contiguous owners of the right half. Leaves apply an epoch to its own owner interval. Every applicable change/owner pair meets once. At one depth, owner intervals are disjoint and the total kernel lengths are $O(n)$ by the arm bound. The middle coefficients of a zero-padded cyclic convolution of width at least owners + kernel length − 1 give the requested correlations. Direct sums over equal kernel plateaus are used only when their cost is within the FFT bound. Thus the time is $O(n\log^2(2n))$ and peak storage $O(n)$ for fixed $b,d$. Bits lacking a repair source share their deletion calculation.

For mixed Q, take $x$ from its longest constant run. Let $m_t$ be the greatest length anywhere in the causal K prefix whose distance from a constant $x$ string is at most one bit. Two suffix counters and a running maximum compute $m_t$ in one pass. If the entire effective query prefix is constant, or its trailing $x$ run has length strictly larger than $m_t$, every original and singly edited winner is determined within the constant suffix. The run algorithm excludes the remaining rows' weights and the actual row sweep adds their exact contributions back. The implementation tries this certificate for a run of at least 16 and accepts up to $1+\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ active exception rows. Scanning all their endpoints costs $O(n\log^2(2n))$ at fixed width. The suffix RMQ can take $O(n\log(2n))$ space here.

## Pack short contexts exactly

Let $M=\max_t L_t$. Each exact arm on either side of a repaired mismatch is itself a causal match. Every repaired length is at most $2M+1$. If that many symbols fit in a machine word, store reversed contexts with their newest symbol in the low bits. XOR and the first differing bit determine the exact suffix length, clipped at the key boundary. An edit to Q toggles the corresponding bit of that context. Radix directories at geometrically growing alphabet sizes locate the best causal occurrence and resolve ties by endpoint. The deepest directory has $O(n)$ buckets for fixed width. Candidates in that directory are checked individually.

A K repair beating an intact winner agrees with its length-$L_t$ tail in all but at most one bit. Enumerate those buckets at the smaller of the directory depth and $L_t$ (one symbol for an empty baseline). A shorter repair can affect only a damaged winner and must end less than $L_t$ positions after its owner. The additional local endpoint window covers all such repairs. Survivor endpoints come from the same staircase and prefix maximum as above. The production path tries widths 1, 2 and 4 and caps bucket work at $O(n\log^2(2n))$. A rejected attempt discards both temporary credit arrays. This path is exact even when many words share a bucket.

The [literal counterfactual oracle](../tests/cpp/subquadratic_counterfactual.cpp) flips the actual input words and recomputes every causal row by dynamic programming. The [paired timing runner](../benchmarks/compare_checkout.py) exercises complete backward through the public NumPy training entry.
